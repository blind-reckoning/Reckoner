The Reckoner sits between the wall buttons and a 230 VAC blind motor. An ESP32-C6 reads the buttons, drives two relays (K1 switches power, K2 picks the direction) and reads motor current and mains voltage from an HLW8110. A LinkSwitch-TN2 buck makes 5 V directly from the mains, with a 3.3 V LDO behind it.

The connectivity is sound: no dangling wires or labels, polarities are correct and the BOM matches. The main findings are one safety point (the board ground is mains live) and a power budget worth confirming.

Limits of this review. The official KiCad ERC could not run: this environment only has KiCad 7, which cannot read the KiCad 10 file and has no ERC command. The connectivity below was rebuilt from the schematic's wires, labels, junctions and pin positions, and checked by script and by reading the design. A real ERC in KiCad 10 should still be run before ordering.

Block diagram

Red lines carry mains. Everything on the board shares one ground, which is the mains live after F1.

Findings

High

Board ground is mains live

GND is the fuse output (F1.2) on the live side, and the neutral is rectified against it. This is normal for a non-isolated buck, but every test point is therefore at mains potential: TP1 (+5V/VBUS), TP2 (GND), TP3 and TP4 (USB), TP5 to TP13. Connecting a PC over USB or a UART adapter with mains applied puts live on the host. The label "VBUS" on TP1 also has no isolation or diode-OR, so an external 5 V would back-feed the buck output.

Action. Program and debug only with mains disconnected, powering the board from a bench 3.3 V supply at TP9 and TP2, or use an isolated adapter. Add a silkscreen warning at the test points. Keep the finished unit fully enclosed in the wall box.

Medium

+5 V budget is tight

The +5 V rail feeds the LNK3206D buck load: K1 coil (200 mW, about 40 mA), K2 coil (400 mW, about 80 mA, held for the whole upward run), the AP2112K and the 1.5 kΩ preload R4. The ESP32-C6 module draws up to 354 mA at TX peak (BOM). L2 is rated Irms 350 mA. The AP2112K drops 1.7 V, so a 350 mA burst dissipates about 0.6 W in a SOT-23-5.

Action. Work out the worst-case 5 V current against the LNK3206D datasheet output-current figure for 230 VAC. Confirm L2 Irms and the LDO thermal limit with that figure. C7 (470 µF) and C5/C6 cover short Wi-Fi bursts; check that the average, not just the peak, fits.

Medium

Relay sequencing is a firmware constraint

K2 is wired so its normally closed contact carries M_DN and its normally open contact carries M_UP (pin 3 is NO, pin 4 is NC in the G5Q-1 symbol). With both coils off the motor is disconnected (K1 open), which is the safe state. K2 is rated 3 A, so it should never change over under load.

Action. Firmware must open K1, wait for the contact to settle, switch K2, then close K1. Never energise K1 while K2 is mid-change. The R8 and R9 100 kΩ pull-downs keep both relays off during reset and boot.

Low

Buck feedback value rests on an assumed VFB

With R2 = 3.3 kΩ and R3 = 2.2 kΩ, the feedback pin sees 40% of the hold-capacitor voltage. D3 and D2 are the same part, so C4 holds about Vout. That gives 5.0 V only if the LNK3206 feedback threshold is 2.0 V. That figure is from memory, not read from the datasheet in this review.

Action. Check the LNK3206 VFB value in the Power Integrations datasheet or PI Expert before ordering.

Low

HLW8110 filter capacitors differ from the datasheet

The datasheet typical circuit uses 33 nF on IAP, IAN and VP. This design uses 100 nF (C12 to C14). Both channels use 1 kΩ and 100 nF, so their phase shift matches and relative phase error stays small.

Action. Fine to keep. Calibrate gain once the unit is built; the longer time constant slightly attenuates 50 Hz.

Low

Strapping and unused pins on the ESP32-C6

IO8 has a 10 kΩ pull-up (R6), as needed for boot. IO9 (BOOT) relies on the module's internal pull-up. IO15 is a strapping pin and is left with a no-connect flag, as are IO4 to IO7 and the module's NC pins.

Action. No change needed. Keep IO15 unused or driven only by firmware after boot.

Low

Symbols stand in for the real parts

U2 (AP2112K-3.3) uses the TPS76316 symbol. U1 (LNK3206D) uses the LNK3202D symbol. Pin numbers match the footprints, and the AP2112K's pin 4 (power-good on the symbol, not connected on the real part) is correctly flagged no-connect.

Action. Rename the symbols or set clear descriptions so the schematic does not mislead a reader or a later ERC.

Note

No snubber or X capacitor

There is no RC snubber across the motor contacts and no X-class capacitor on the mains input. A snubber can reduce contact wear on an inductive motor. EMC and safety approval are out of scope for a hobby build but worth a thought before wider use.

Action. Optional. Leave footprints in the layout if board space allows.

Checks that passed

  • Every net has at least two pins, apart from the 12 deliberate no-connects. No dangling wires, no dangling labels, no unwired pins.
  • Power flags: +5 V (FLG02), GND (FLG01) and the SW node (FLG03) are present. Every power-input pin is on a net that has a driver.
  • Schematic and bom.csv agree on all designators, values, footprints and LCSC numbers (0 mismatches, 0 missing).
  • Footprint pads match symbol pins for all parts whose footprint exists in the KiCad 7 library. The ESP32-C6-MINI-1 footprint is not in that library, so it was not checked.
  • Buck stage: M7 rectifier, 10 Ω fusible resistor, C1, L1 and C2 pi filter, D2 freewheel diode with the cathode on SW, L2 and the D3/C4 feedback hold circuit all have the correct polarity.
  • Fuse F1 is ahead of the varistor RV1; the varistor sits across live and neutral after the fuse.
  • Relay drivers: AO3400A gate, source, drain order is correct; flyback diodes D5 and D6 have the cathode on +5 V; 100 kΩ gate pull-downs keep the relays off at reset.
  • HLW8110 pins match the datasheet (1 IAP, 2 IAN, 3 VP, 4 GND, 5 VREF, 6 TX, 7 RX, 8 VDD). UART lines are crossed correctly (MTR_RX to the HLW8110 TX, MTR_TX to its RX). The shunt R10 sits upstream of the relays, so it sees the whole motor current. Voltage divider ratio is about 1 : 2001, giving roughly 0.16 V peak at 230 VAC.
  • Input clamps D7 to D9 (BAT54S) are oriented correctly: one diode to GND, one to +3V3. Series resistors are 1206 parts (100 kΩ and 1 MΩ, 200 V rated), each seeing at most half the line peak.
  • Both relays are rated for the load in the install diagram (about 1 A, 230 VAC): K1 10 A, K2 3 A.
